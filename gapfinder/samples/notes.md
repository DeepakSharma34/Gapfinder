# Cell biology

The cell membrane is selectively permeable and controls movement of substances into and out of the cell. Diffusion moves particles from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration without requiring metabolic energy. Osmosis is the movement of water across a selectively permeable membrane from higher water potential to lower water potential.

Mitochondria are the main sites of aerobic respiration in eukaryotic cells. Ribosomes synthesize proteins by translating messenger RNA. The nucleus contains genetic material and regulates gene expression. Active transport moves substances against their concentration gradient using energy supplied by ATP.

# Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis converts light energy into chemical energy stored in organic molecules. Chlorophyll absorbs light in chloroplasts. The light-dependent reactions occur in the thylakoid membranes and produce ATP and NADPH. Water is split during these reactions, releasing oxygen as a byproduct.

The Calvin cycle occurs in the stroma of the chloroplast. It uses ATP and NADPH from the light-dependent reactions to support carbon fixation. Carbon dioxide supplies the carbon incorporated into organic molecules. Light intensity, carbon dioxide availability, and temperature can limit the rate of photosynthesis.

# Cellular respiration

Cellular respiration transfers energy from glucose to ATP. Glycolysis occurs in the cytoplasm and splits one glucose molecule into two pyruvate molecules, producing a net gain of two ATP molecules. Glycolysis does not directly require oxygen.

The Krebs cycle occurs in the mitochondrial matrix. The electron transport chain is located in the inner mitochondrial membrane. Oxygen acts as the final electron acceptor in aerobic respiration. A proton gradient drives ATP synthase to produce ATP during oxidative phosphorylation.

# Genetics

DNA stores hereditary information in its sequence of nucleotides. A gene is a segment of DNA associated with a functional product. Different versions of a gene are called alleles. A genotype describes an organism's genetic constitution, while a phenotype describes observable characteristics influenced by genotype and environment.

Meiosis reduces chromosome number by half and produces haploid cells. Crossing over during prophase of meiosis I exchanges genetic material between homologous chromosomes. Independent assortment and crossing over increase genetic variation. In a simple monohybrid cross between two heterozygotes with complete dominance, the expected phenotypic ratio is three dominant to one recessive.

# Ecology

An ecosystem consists of living organisms and their physical environment interacting together. Producers convert energy into organic matter, while consumers obtain energy by feeding on other organisms. Decomposers break down dead organic material and recycle nutrients into the ecosystem.

Energy flows through trophic levels and some energy is lost as heat at each transfer. Nutrients are recycled through biogeochemical cycles. A population consists of organisms of the same species living in an area. Carrying capacity is the population size an environment can sustain over time with available resources.

# Human physiology

Alveoli provide a large surface area for gas exchange in the lungs. Their thin walls and a rich capillary supply help oxygen diffuse into the blood and carbon dioxide diffuse out. Hemoglobin in red blood cells binds and transports oxygen.

The nephron is the functional unit of the kidney. Ultrafiltration occurs at the glomerulus, while selective reabsorption returns useful substances to the blood. Insulin lowers blood glucose by promoting glucose uptake and storage. Glucagon raises blood glucose by stimulating release of glucose from liver stores. Negative feedback helps maintain stable internal conditions.
